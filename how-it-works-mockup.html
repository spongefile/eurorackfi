For owners

How this works

Before you send us a list, here is what belongs here, how to get started, and exactly what we hold.

What belongs here

Yes

  • Eurorack modules and cases
  • Desktop and standalone gear
  • Accessories — cables, power, tools, panels
  • Odd sound things: strange headphones,
    contact mics, unusual pedals

No

  • Anything unrelated to sound
  • Generic electronics anyone sells
  • Bulk or trade quantities

The test is whether someone in the Finnish eurorack group would stop scrolling at it. Weird headphones, yes. Baby clothes, no. A generic alarm clock, no — a strange one someone built, probably yes. Suggest whatever you like. In the end we decide, because we built this and would rather not spend the evenings arguing about it.

Getting started

1

You send us

  • A list of your modules — including ones you are not selling yet. Everything arrives hidden, so nothing goes public until you say so.
  • Prices for the ones you would sell.
  • A wishlist — what you are looking for.
  • A way for buyers to reach you. A Tally form of your own — enquiries land in your Tally inbox, and by email too if you switch notifications on. Or honestly any page where someone can contact you — your own site's contact form is fine.

    Whatever form you use

    Include a question asking which module they mean. It is the one thing tying an enquiry to an item. Without it, messages arrive reading “is this still available?” with no way to tell what this is. Our side helps: the contact link on each module page shows the buyer that module’s name, so it is sitting in front of them to copy.

    Four questions is plenty:

    1. Which module are you asking about? — the one that matters
    2. Your name
    3. Your email — so you can reply
    4. Your message

    No form yet? tally.so is free and takes about five minutes. Make an account, add those four questions, publish, and send us the link. Switch on email notifications while you are there, or you will only see enquiries when you next open it.

  • Your email address, so we can send you your link. This one is for us, not for buyers.

Send it all through the contact form on eurorack.fi.

2

We do the work

  • We add every module's details — photos, specs, HP, power, panel drawing, and the description in three languages.
  • We send you your own link.
3

You go live

  • Unhide whatever you actually want to sell.
  • From then on it is yours — sold, negotiation, prices, wishlist.

Two different contacts, and they are not the same thing. The contact form is public and is how buyers reach you — we never see those messages. Your email address is private and is only used to send you your link.

Adding something new later — an item or a wishlist entry — means asking us, and we deal with those in batches every few weeks rather than daily. Everything you can change yourself is instant. It is only new content that waits.

Where things live

On eurorack.fi

public, in the open
  • Your name and colour
  • Your items — photos, specs, prices
  • Your wishlist
  • What is sold, hidden, or in negotiation
→

On Tally

your own form, not us
  • Buyers' names and emails
  • What they wrote to you
  • Which item they asked about

We hold no buyer details at all. Messages go through your own form straight to your Tally inbox — we never see them. Nothing about a buyer is ever stored on this site, so there is nothing here to leak, sell, or ask us to delete.

What you change, and what you ask for

Instantly, from your own link no login, works on a phone
  • Mark something sold
  • Mark something in negotiation
  • Change a price
  • Say a price is negotiable
  • Hide a wishlist entry you have found
  • Put any of it back
By asking us a form on the same page
  • Adding a new item
  • Adding a wishlist entry
  • Changing a photo or description
  • Fixing specs, HP, or power draw

You control the facts only you know — what sold, what it costs, whether you will haggle. We handle the parts that need work — photographing it, checking the specs, drawing the panel to scale, writing it in three languages.

Why it is built this way

This is a hobby project. A couple of people, evenings.

There are two ways to build something like this. One assumes nobody can be trusted: accounts, passwords, moderation, spam defences, and a plan for the day someone abuses it. That is a swamp of a project that never stops asking for attention.

The other assumes you can be trusted, because we know who you are. It is a fraction of the work and it stays small.

We are doing the second one — and honestly, the first one would not exist. Not because it is a worse idea, but because nobody here wants to spend their evenings running it.

That is also why we ask for more than eight modules, and why someone vouches for you if we do not know you. That trust is doing the job a login screen would otherwise be doing. It is not gatekeeping for its own sake — it is the thing holding the whole arrangement up.

What you get out of it: no account to make, no password to forget, one link that works on a phone. What you give up: you cannot add or rewrite content yourself, and new things wait for us.

Made with AI

This entire project has been made with AI. That means there will be inevitable mistakes and inaccuracies — but we hope the human and personal component of this (the fact that we are a community and not strangers on the internet) will smooth over those “the site has the wrong colour panel shown” moments.

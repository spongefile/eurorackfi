Hero rack — the rails problem, and three ways to read as a case

Measured on the live site at 1280: the rack box is 639px wide, the rails are 637px, but row 2's modules total 778px and row 3's total 724px. The rows overflow the box while the rails stop at its edge — so scrolling right shows photos continuing past where the rails end. Meanwhile the 1U row totals only 321px and simply stops halfway, leaving bare background. Both are the same underlying thing: rails are sized to the container, rows are sized to their content.

A — Current

Rows stop where the photos stop; rails run to the container edge. Reads as photographs laid between two horizontal bars rather than modules in a rack.

B — Rails follow the content, empty slots get blanking panels

One CSS change fixes the rails: an inner wrapper at width:max-content; min-width:100% makes rails and rows share the same width, whichever is wider. Empty slots fill with a blanking panel — brushed metal with the screw positions — which is literally what a half-full case looks like. Nothing stops mid-air any more.

C — B, plus case cheeks

Adds the two things that actually say "case": dark end cheeks with mounting screws, and a dark body behind the rails so the rails read as metal inside an enclosure rather than as grey bars on the page background. The modules are unchanged — the enclosure does the work. Costs a darker block in the hero, which is a real trade against the light palette.

Blanking panel — the filler was too light

A filler lighter than the modules reads as sitting in front of them. Both of these are darker so the empty slot recedes. Left: branded, an eurorack.fi cover panel in the house blue with the jack mark tiled quietly down it. Right: a plain dark anodised blank.

E — Wood cheeks

Warmth against an otherwise very pale, very blue site. Uses the palette's existing wood faceplate tone rather than introducing a new colour — Bastl panels already carry it, so the hero and the cards stay related.

Wood cheeks + branded blue blanks. The blue blank now reads as belonging to the case rather than floating in front of it, and the wood gives the hero something warm to sit against the blue-grey page.
